7. Key concepts

Relation

A table that stores data about one kind of thing, made of rows and columns. Example: STUDENT.

Attribute

A column of a relation that describes one property. Example: Email in STUDENT.

Tuple

A single row of a relation. Example: (101, Ravi Kumar, ravi.kumar@example.com, Computer Science).

Relational schema

The blueprint of the database: relation names, their attributes and keys, without the data.

# Student Course Management - Relational Schema

A relational database schema design for a Student Course Management System.
It stores information about students, courses and student course registrations.

## Relations

| Relation   | Attributes                                          | Primary Key   |
|------------|-----------------------------------------------------|---------------|
| STUDENT    | Student_ID, Student_Name, Email, Department         | Student_ID    |
| COURSE     | Course_ID, Course_Name, Trainer_Name, Duration      | Course_ID     |
| ENROLLMENT | Enrollment_ID, Student_ID, Course_ID, Enrollment_Date | Enrollment_ID |

## What is a relation?
A relation is a table that stores data about one kind of entity. It is made of rows and columns.

## What is an attribute?
An attribute is a column of a relation. It describes one property of the entity, such as Email.

## What is a tuple?
A tuple is a single row in a relation. It holds one complete record, such as one student.

## What is a relational schema?
A relational schema is the structure of the database. It lists the relation names, their attributes and their primary keys.

## Primary key used in each relation
- STUDENT: Student_ID
- COURSE: Course_ID
- ENROLLMENT: Enrollment_ID

## Foreign keys in ENROLLMENT
- Student_ID refers to STUDENT(Student_ID)
- Course_ID refers to COURSE(Course_ID)

## Relationship
One student can enroll in many courses, and one course can have many students.
ENROLLMENT connects STUDENT and COURSE and turns this many-to-many
relationship into two one-to-many relationships.
